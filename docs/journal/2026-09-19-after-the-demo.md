## Standing questions

### Would I actually use this?

Not yet, and that is still the honest answer. I mix in Ableton. I reach for tools that already live there. A plugin that only sees one track is already the wrong shape for how I think about mixing — mixing is tracks fitting together, not a vocal in a vacuum. I would use a copilot that stays in Ableton and eventually sees the session. I would not switch DAWs for this, and I would not ask collaborators to switch. That resistance is about workflow, not about “building a DAW is hard.”

### What is the use case?

The use case I can stand behind today is still the original one: I know how I want something to *sound* and I don’t want to hunt the tool. “This vocal is muddy” while I am in a session, in Ableton, on a clock. The use case people keep pointing at is bigger: a ground-up AI copilot, not another chat bolted onto a rack. Producer Pal and Ableton MCP-style tools exist. The gap is designing the copilot native, not wrapping someone else’s session. I have not proven I would use even the small version.

There is a later use case I can already feel: when one-track context runs out, the product becomes “make these things sit together.” That is mixing. I am not there yet.

### Is this cheating?

The fear is not “EQ without turning a knob.” The fear is whether AI belongs in creativity at all — whether people will want technical automation, or just flinch. I am already second-guessing that while I build. Saving chains, learning from my results over time, training on what I keep vs undo: that is where it stops being a shortcut and starts being a taste engine. I don’t know yet if that is amplifying my ear or replacing it. Speed is fine. Outsourcing taste is the line I have not drawn.

### Should this exist?

I showed a working demo. The reaction was excitement, not politeness. That does not answer the question, but it means I am not the only one who can see a product here. I still don’t know what I want: open source, get paid, or turn it into a company. Those are different moral objects. A local assistant that explains itself and stays reversible is easier to defend than a cloud model that learns my mix taste and leaves the machine. Failure modes I already care about: people afraid of it, people skipping the craft, the tool being too confident, me building a DAW nobody will move to.

### What did I just give up?

By staying a plugin in someone else’s DAW, I give up session context — and I can already tell that limits the intelligence. By not becoming a DAW, I keep Ableton, collaborators, and a life. If I chase a new DAW, I give up years and the users I already have (myself). If I stay a blind single-track rack, I give up the actual job of mixing.

### What would make me uninstall it?

If it is slower than grabbing Pro-Q. If it only ever sees one track and keeps guessing. If using it means leaving Ableton. If it starts making taste decisions I cannot see or undo. If the latency makes chat feel like waiting instead of mixing.
